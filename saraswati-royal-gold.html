Normalization & Functional Dependencies

A relation is in Third Normal Form (3NF) if it is in 2NF and every non-prime attribute is non-transitively dependent on every candidate key. This removes redundancy caused by transitive dependencies.

Formally, a functional dependency X → Y holds if, for any two tuples with the same value of X, the value of Y is also identical. This is the foundation used to derive candidate keys.

R is in 3NF ⇔ ∀ X→A: X is a superkey OR A is prime

Decomposing a relation to remove transitive dependencies typically involves splitting it into two relations connected by the determinant attribute, preserving both dependency and lossless-join properties.

Why do we need 3NF if 2NF already removes partial dependencies?
2NF only eliminates dependencies on part of a composite key. 3NF goes further and removes transitive dependencies — where a non-key attribute depends on another non-key attribute rather than the key directly.
